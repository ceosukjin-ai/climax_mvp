## 1. Methods — 새로 넣을 문단 (뷰팩터 정의)

> **View factors.** Sky, tree and building view factors (SVF, TVF, BVF) were derived from
> the 360° panorama taken at the globe position. Each panorama was stitched from the
> camera's two fisheye images with a per-image attitude correction, projected onto four
> horizontal and four 45°-tilted perspective faces plus a nadir face, and segmented with
> SegFormer-B0 trained on ADE20K. Pixel labels were resampled to a 1° zenith–azimuth grid
> and integrated over the upper hemisphere with the same cosine weighting (Steyn, 1980),
> so the three factors share one definition and, with a residual class (poles, signs,
> vehicles; median 0.02), sum to one (Gong et al., 2018). No empirical scaling was
> applied. The panorama at one site could not be oriented and was excluded, leaving
> 79 sites for analyses involving view factors.

## 2. Methods — 부지 선정 문단 (표고 교체)

> ... LCZ class is not separable from site-specific factors such as elevation
> (**4–9 m** in LCZ 4 against **51–130 m** in LCZ 2; Copernicus 90 m DEM) and distance to
> the coast, so the between-class term is a combined local effect rather than an LCZ
> effect alone.

## 3. Results 5.1 — 형태 4패널

**Setting the comparison** — 바뀐 숫자 없음. 그대로 둔다.

**(a) Street width**, **(b) Height-to-width ratio** — 바뀐 숫자 없음. 그대로 둔다.

**(c) Sky view factor** — 전면 교체:

> **(c) Sky view factor.** Across all sites sky view factor was the strongest of the three
> indices (ρ = +0.46, *p* < 0.001, n = 79), but like the aspect ratio it was carried by
> exposure. Among sunlit sites the relation weakened to ρ = +0.20 (*p* = 0.12, n = 61),
> and ranking within LCZ classes did not recover it (*p* = 0.11). Open sites are more often
> sunlit; once a pedestrian is in the beam, sky openness adds little.

**(d) Sky view factor and radiant load** — 숫자 교체:

> **(d) Sky view factor and radiant load.** Panel (d) plots the same sky view factor
> against mean radiant temperature. Over all sites the two are correlated (ρ = +0.33,
> *p* = 0.003, n = 79), but among sunlit sites — where the beam is held constant — the
> correlation vanishes (ρ = −0.01, *p* = 0.96), and ranking within LCZ classes leaves
> nothing (*p* = 0.88). Sky view factor tracks radiant load only in so far as it separates
> sun from shade. Once the direct beam reaches a point, the beam dominates the radiant
> load and the second-order effect of sky openness on diffuse and long-wave exchange falls
> below the measurement resolution.

(옛 문장 「The residual association with PET in (c) follows air temperature rather than
radiation (Supplementary S2)」 는 **삭제** — (c) 에 볕 지점 잔여 관계가 더는 없다.)

**The three shaded sites above threshold** — 바뀐 숫자 없음. 그대로 둔다.

**Taken together** — 교체:

> **Taken together.** Entered jointly for sunlit sites, the three indices explained 3 % of
> the variance in PET (R² = 0.033, adjusted −0.023, n = 55); street width and aspect ratio
> were collinear (ρ = −0.87) and only moderately related to sky view factor (ρ = +0.28 and
> −0.38), and no variance inflation factor exceeded 1.5. None of the three indices, alone
> or together, substitutes for whether the beam reaches the location. The LCZ scheme
> therefore remains a valid description of the local thermal environment while being
> insufficient for the pedestrian standing in it: two points 10 m apart on the same street,
> one under a street tree and one beside it, share LCZ class, street width, aspect ratio
> and land-cover class, yet differ by approximately the 7 K reported above. For the
> population addressed here — older adults living alone, who walk a small number of fixed
> routes near home — that difference is the exposure.

> **S2.** Among sunlit sites sky view factor was weakly associated with air temperature

## 5. Results 5.1 — 결정요인 문단 (순열검정)

> Sky view factor on its own was related to mean radiant temperature within
> neighbourhoods (r = 0.31, within-neighbourhood permutation *p* = 0.008), but with
> surface temperature and sun exposure in the model it was no longer significant
> (*p* = 0.48); the sun term carried the effect (+11.5 K, *p* < 0.001). Pavement surface
> temperature was related to PET within neighbourhoods (r = 0.51, *p* < 0.001).

## 6. Results — Fig 6 (b) 문단 (신규)

> To compare the neighbourhoods on a common footing, every site was placed at one
> reference condition — the campaign medians: 23 August 12:30, solar elevation 65°,
> air temperature 35.0 °C, relative humidity 47.6 %, pedestrian wind 0.5 m s⁻¹, clear
> sky — keeping only its measured view factors and its sun-or-shade reading, and PET was
> recomputed with the same engine (Fig. 6b). Sunlit sites then ranged from 44.4 to
> 46.9 °C and shaded sites from 40.5 to 41.9 °C, with no overlap (gap 2.4 K). The shade
> benefit was 4.5–5.1 K in every neighbourhood with shaded sites. The engine gives a
> smaller sun–shade difference than the field data (median 4.6 against 5.4 K), so
> Fig. 6b is read for the comparison between neighbourhoods rather than for the absolute
> level. Under median campaign weather, 17 of the 19 shaded sites still sat at
> 41–42 °C — at the threshold rather than clearly below it — so shade alone did not remove
> extreme heat stress on a typical afternoon.
