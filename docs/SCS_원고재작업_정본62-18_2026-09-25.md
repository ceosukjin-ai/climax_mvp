## 1. Methods

### 1.1 볕/그늘 라벨 (2.2.4 또는 4.3 — 기존 "Sun/shade label" 문단 전면 교체)

> **Sun/shade label.** Direct-beam exposure at the globe position was read from the 360° panorama taken at the same point within one minute of the globe reading. A site was labelled sunlit when the observer standing beside the tripod, or objects within 2 m of it, cast a hard shadow; overcast or cloud-diffuse conditions, in which no hard shadow was visible anywhere in the panorama, were labelled shaded, because the quantity of interest is the direct beam rather than sky brightness. Two readers labelled all 80 sites independently (agreement 76/80); the four disagreements were resolved by joint re-inspection of the region of the panorama nearest the tripod, using the observer's shadow as the criterion. The final labels were 62 sunlit and 18 shaded sites, and were cross-checked against the globe-temperature rise above air temperature (21.7 K sunlit against 9.4 K shaded, Mann–Whitney *p* < 0.001). Automatic detection of the solar disc in the panorama, used in an earlier version, misjudged 12 of the 80 sites where the disc fell on the stitching seam or bloomed into the sky, and was abandoned.

### 1.2 뷰팩터 정의 (2.3.x — 신규 문단, 09-21 판과 같음)

> **View factors.** Sky, tree and building view factors (SVF, TVF, BVF) were derived from the 360° panorama taken at the globe position. Each panorama was stitched from the camera's two fisheye images with a per-image attitude correction, projected onto four horizontal and four 45°-tilted perspective faces plus a nadir face, and segmented with SegFormer-B0 trained on ADE20K. Pixel labels were resampled to a 1° zenith–azimuth grid and integrated over the upper hemisphere with the same cosine weighting (Steyn, 1980), so the three factors share one definition and, with a residual class (poles, signs, vehicles; median 0.02), sum to one (Gong et al., 2018). No empirical scaling was applied. The panorama at one site could not be oriented and was excluded, leaving 79 sites for analyses involving view factors. Sky view factor ranged from 0.01 (a covered walkway) to 0.85 (median 0.59).

### 1.3 부지 선정 · 표고 (4.1 — 한 문장 교체)

> ... LCZ class is not separable from site-specific factors such as elevation (**4–9 m** in LCZ 4 against **51–130 m** in LCZ 2; Copernicus 90 m DEM) and distance to the coast, so the between-class term is a combined local effect rather than an LCZ effect alone.

### 1.4 가로 기하 · 측정 조건 (4.3 / 6.5 — 유지, 숫자 확인됨)

> **Street geometry.** Street width and building height were derived geometrically from building footprints and the road centreline. Width could not be derived at seven of the 80 sites (no road centreline within the search radius), so analyses involving width or aspect ratio are based on 73 sites (56 sunlit).

> **Measurement regime.** Observations were made on four clear summer afternoons (20, 23, 25 and 26 August 2026) at solar elevations of 46–67°, air temperatures of 32.0–38.7 °C and wind speeds of 0.0–2.2 m s⁻¹. The sampled canyons are shallow (median H/W 0.54; 12 of 73 sites at H/W ≥ 1 and five at ≥ 2).

## 2. Results 5.1 — 볕/그늘과 도시형태 (전면 교체)

### Urban form indices separate heat between LCZ classes, not within them

> **Setting the comparison.** Direct-beam exposure at the sensor was the dominant control on thermal stress. Sunlit sites averaged PET 47.8 ± 2.7 °C (n = 62) against 40.3 ± 3.2 °C at shaded sites (n = 18), a difference of 7.4 K in the mean and 7.7 K in the median (Mann–Whitney *p* < 10⁻⁷; Cliff's δ = 0.86). The separation was larger in mean radiant temperature, 57.2 ± 6.9 °C against 43.7 ± 4.7 °C (13.5 K, δ = 0.92), while air temperature differed by only 1.2 K. Sixty-one of the 62 sunlit sites (98 %) exceeded the extreme-heat-stress threshold of PET 41 °C, against two of 18 shaded sites (11 %). Most of this variation occurred inside the LCZ classes rather than between them: the five classes accounted for 31 % of the variance in PET, leaving 69 % within classes, and the widest single class spanned 15.5 K (LCZ 5, 37.6–53.1 °C) against the 6.3 K separating the five class means. Within each class the separation was again the direct beam (+7.7 K in LCZ 5, +7.1 K in LCZ 2 and LCZ 3; LCZ 4 contained no shaded site, and LCZ 1 only two). Figure X asks whether the three canyon form indices in common use can reproduce that within-class structure.

> **(a) Street width.** Street width ranged from 2.2 to 66.1 m and was unrelated to PET (Spearman ρ = +0.12, *p* = 0.31, n = 73). Restricting the comparison to sunlit sites, where the beam is held constant, left nothing (ρ = +0.05, *p* = 0.72, n = 56), and ranking within LCZ classes did not recover it (*p* = 0.08). What the panel does show is a horizontal separation by exposure: shaded sites lie almost entirely below the 41 °C threshold and sunlit sites almost entirely above it, at every street width.

> **(b) Height-to-width ratio.** Aspect ratio was weakly negative across all sites (ρ = −0.24, *p* = 0.04, n = 73), the direction expected from canyon theory. The relation did not survive conditioning. Among sunlit sites it disappeared (ρ = −0.14, *p* = 0.29, n = 56), and within LCZ classes it was absent (*p* = 0.50). The panel makes the reason visible: shaded sites concentrate at high aspect ratios, so the pooled correlation registers that deep canyons are more often shaded, not that canyon geometry lowers heat load once a pedestrian is in the beam.

> **(c) Sky view factor.** Across all sites sky view factor was the strongest of the three indices (ρ = +0.46, *p* < 0.001, n = 79), but like the aspect ratio it was carried by exposure. Among sunlit sites the relation weakened to ρ = +0.21 (*p* = 0.10, n = 62), and ranking within LCZ classes did not recover it (*p* = 0.07). Open sites are more often sunlit; once a pedestrian is in the beam, sky openness adds little.

> **(d) Sky view factor and radiant load.** Panel (d) plots the same sky view factor against mean radiant temperature. Over all sites the two are correlated (ρ = +0.33, *p* = 0.003, n = 79), but among sunlit sites — where the beam is held constant — the correlation vanishes (ρ = −0.00, *p* = 0.98), and ranking within LCZ classes leaves nothing (*p* = 0.82). Sky view factor tracks radiant load only in so far as it separates sun from shade. Once the direct beam reaches a point, the beam dominates the radiant load and the second-order effect of sky openness on diffuse and long-wave exchange falls below the measurement resolution.

> **The two shaded sites above threshold.** Panel (c) also shows that shade was not uniformly protective. The two shaded sites exceeding PET 41 °C were both recorded in LCZ 1 between 15:00 and 15:06 on the hottest afternoon, at air temperatures of 38.3–38.7 °C, reaching PET 47.7–49.0 °C. Their globe-temperature rise (10.9–12.7 K) confirms the shade classification against the sunlit mean of 21.7 K. Shade removes the radiant load but not the air temperature, so on the hottest afternoon building shade alone left pedestrians in the extreme-stress range.

> **Taken together.** Entered jointly for sunlit sites, the three indices explained 3 % of the variance in PET (R² = 0.034, adjusted −0.022, n = 56); street width and aspect ratio were collinear (ρ = −0.87) and only moderately related to sky view factor (ρ = +0.28 and −0.38), and no variance inflation factor exceeded 1.5. None of the three indices, alone or together, substitutes for whether the beam reaches the location. The LCZ scheme therefore remains a valid description of the local thermal environment while being insufficient for the pedestrian standing in it: two points 10 m apart on the same street, one under a street tree and one beside it, share LCZ class, street width, aspect ratio and land-cover class, yet differ by approximately the 7 K reported above. For the population addressed here — older adults living alone, who walk a small number of fixed routes near home — that difference is the exposure.

## 4. Results — Fig 6 (b) 기준조건 문단

(62/18 로 재실행한 `ref_condition_80.csv` 기준 — 확정.)

> To compare the neighbourhoods on a common footing, every site was placed at one reference condition — the campaign medians: 23 August 12:30, solar elevation 65°, air temperature 35.0 °C, relative humidity 47.6 %, pedestrian wind 0.5 m s⁻¹, clear sky — keeping only its measured view factors and its sun-or-shade reading, and PET was recomputed with the same engine (Fig. 6b). Sunlit sites then ranged from 44.4 to 46.9 °C and shaded sites from 40.5 to 41.9 °C, with no overlap (gap 2.4 K). The shade benefit was 4.4–5.1 K in every neighbourhood with shaded sites. The engine gives a smaller sun–shade difference than the field data (per-neighbourhood mean difference, median 4.6 against 7.1 K), so Fig. 6b is read for the comparison between neighbourhoods rather than for the absolute level. Under median campaign weather, 16 of the 18 shaded sites still sat at 41–42 °C — at the threshold rather than clearly below it — so shade alone did not remove extreme heat stress on a typical afternoon.

> **S2.** Among sunlit sites sky view factor was weakly associated with air temperature (ρ = +0.25, *p* = 0.05, n = 62). ## 7. Limitations (6.5) — 추가 문장

> **Sun/shade labelling.** The sun/shade label is a binary reading of one panorama per site. Two readers agreed at 76 of 80 sites; the four disagreements, all at sites where the beam grazed the tripod or the sky was partly clouded, were settled by the observer's shadow. Reassigning the one site that remained borderline (globe rise 17.5 K, between the sunlit and shaded means) to shade changes no conclusion (sunlit-only SVF–PET ρ = +0.20, *p* = 0.12; within-class *p* = 0.11).

> **Statistical power.** The within-class test has 62 % power for a true ρ of 0.3 at n = 62 across five classes, so the null result is a statement about detectability in this sample rather than evidence of absence.

> **Sampled geometry.** The conclusion is that the beam overwhelms canyon geometry under high-sun summer conditions in low-rise streets, not that aspect ratio is irrelevant in general; deep canyons (H/W ≥ 2 at five sites) and low solar elevations were not sampled.
